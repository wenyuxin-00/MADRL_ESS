\section{Title of Master Thesis}

The title of the thesis is: Building Energy Management System Based on Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning.

This thesis investigates coordinated energy management for multiple prosumers in a low-voltage distribution network. Each prosumer has household loads, rooftop PV, a stationary battery, and residential EV charging demand. The objective is to coordinate battery operation, PV utilization, and EV charging under dynamic electricity prices. At the same time, the controller should meet user mobility needs and distribution network safety constraints.

\section{Abstract}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}

Residential PV systems, stationary battery energy storage systems, and electric vehicles are becoming common in distribution networks. As a result, residential users are changing from passive electricity consumers into prosumers. They can generate electricity, store energy, and adjust part of their demand. In a single household, PV generation can reduce electricity purchased from the grid. The battery can be charged during low-price periods and discharged during high-price periods. EV charging can also be scheduled according to mobility needs and electricity prices. When several prosumers are connected to the same low-voltage network, these local decisions are no longer only household-level economic decisions. EV charging, battery operation, and PV utilization change the feeder net load together. This can affect distribution network safety.

HEMS is an important tool for demand-side management and renewable energy use. Existing HEMS studies have discussed energy management architectures, device models, and scheduling strategies (Zhou et al., 2016; Beaudin and Zareipour, 2015; Ali et al., 2022). MPC and optimization-based methods can handle device constraints and forecast information in an explicit way. They have shown good performance in residential energy storage, EV scheduling, and demand response (Chen et al., 2013; Jin et al., 2017; Seal et al., 2023). However, the problem becomes larger when multiple prosumers, EVs, BESSs, PV systems, dynamic prices, and grid constraints are considered together. The computational burden also increases. Reinforcement learning and multi-agent deep reinforcement learning offer another approach. They can learn online scheduling policies through interaction with the environment and support centralized training with decentralized execution (Xu et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024).

\subsection{Problem Statement and Objective}

This thesis tend to solve the following research problem: how can stationary batteries, PV systems, and EV charging be coordinated among multiple prosumers in a low-voltage distribution network? The controller should reduce operating cost, satisfy the EV departure SoC requirement, and reduce distribution network safety issues. The problem has temporal and multi-agent coupling. Battery SoC and EV SoC make current actions influence future feasible actions. In addition, the actions of several prosumers affect the network operating state through power flow calculations.

To address this problem, this thesis develops a low-voltage distribution network simulation environment based on pandapower. The problem is then studied with a MADRL-based approach. The proposed method is compared with baseline controllers. The evaluation uses total cost, battery arbitrage profit, EV charging cost, departure SoC feasibility, and distribution network safety.

\subsection{Research Questions and Hypotheses}

The research question is: Can MADRL learn a control policy for a multi-prosumer low-voltage distribution network with EVs, BESSs, and PV systems? The policy should reduce cost, satisfy EV mobility requirements, and maintain distribution network safety.

Based on this research question, the following hypotheses are proposed before conducting the study:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compared with rule-based controllers, MADRL can use electricity prices, SoC, PV generation, and load demand to learn a more flexible resource-coupling policy. This may improve EV charging cost and battery arbitrage performance.
  \item A reward function that only uses the EV departure penalty may suffer from sparse rewards. It may therefore fail to satisfy the EV departure SoC requirement in a stable way. Additional reward components are needed to improve EV feasibility.
  \item EV hard correction can enforce EV mobility feasibility at the action execution layer. However, the EV constraint alone cannot ensure distribution network safety. Grid safety projection can reduce voltage violations and transformer overloading, but it may reduce battery arbitrage profit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Planned Methodology}

This study is planned as follows. First, a simulation environment with three prosumers will be built. It will use a SimBench low-voltage distribution network topology and pandapower power flow calculations. Each prosumer includes household load, PV generation, a 100 kWh stationary battery, and one 60 kWh EV. The EV is assumed to arrive home at 18:00 and depart at 07:00. Its arrival SoC is 0.30, and its target departure SoC is 0.90. Second, an LSTM model will forecast electricity prices, load demand, and PV generation. These forecasts provide horizon information for about the next 12 hours. Third, a MADRL controller will be developed. Each agent outputs a three-dimensional continuous action for battery power, PV utilization, and EV charging power. Training follows centralized training and decentralized execution. The critic uses the joint state and joint actions, while the actor uses local observations and forecast sequences during execution. Fourth, several constraint-handling mechanisms will be introduced. These include a soft EV penalty, Progress penalty, hard EV correction, emergency charging, and grid safety projection. Their effects will then be analyzed. Finally, MADRL will be compared with baseline control methods.

\subsection{Expected Results}

The expected outcome of this study is to show the advantages of MADRL over rule-based methods in a multi-prosumer scenario. The study will also examine how EV constraint handling and grid safety projection affect the learned policy. The thesis is expected to make four contributions. First, it will establish a simulation framework that integrates EVs, BESSs, PV systems, a forecasting module, and power flow feedback. Second, it will propose and evaluate constraint-handling approaches for the EV departure SoC requirement. Third, it will quantify the trade-offs between MADRL and baseline controllers. The main criteria are economic performance, EV feasibility, and distribution network safety. Fourth, it will provide an experimental basis for future work on projection-aware training, robust forecasting, and larger multi-prosumer distribution networks.

\section{Proposed Outline of the Thesis}

The proposed structure of this thesis is as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Introduction}: Introduces the background of coordinated control for residential distributed energy resources (DERs), EVs, and low-voltage distribution networks. It presents the economic and grid safety challenges of multi-prosumer energy management and outlines the thesis structure.
  \item \textbf{State of the Art}: Reviews the literature on HEMS, MPC, DRL/MADRL, EV charging scheduling, and distribution network safety constraints. It also identifies the research gaps between single-household HEMS, optimization-based methods, and safe MADRL.
  \item \textbf{System Modelling and Problem Formulation}: Develops the models of prosumers, PV systems, BESSs, EVs, electricity prices, net load, and pandapower power flow. It also defines voltage, line, and transformer constraints, together with the optimization objective.
  \item \textbf{Methodology}: Presents the LSTM forecasting model and the observation, action, and reward design of MADRL. It also describes grid safety projection, EV soft/hard/emergency constraint handling, and the Local MPC, ADMM-MPC, and rule-based baseline controllers.
  \item \textbf{Experimental Setup}: Describes the data sources, training and evaluation periods, device parameters, network configuration, training settings, and evaluation metrics.
  \item \textbf{Results and Discussion}: Analyzes the LSTM forecasting accuracy, MADRL convergence, and the results under different EV constraint-handling strategies. It also discusses the impact of grid safety projection and compares the controllers with MPC and rule-based methods.
  \item \textbf{Conclusion and Outlook}: Summarizes the main findings and discusses future research directions. These include projection-aware MADRL, robust forecasting, larger distribution networks, and hybrid control methods.
\end{enumerate}
